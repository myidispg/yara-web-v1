Next.js migration strategy and things to remember:

This is a brilliant architectural question. Gold rate volatility is exactly the kind of edge case that separates a basic e-commerce site from an enterprise-grade platform. 

The short answer is: **No, it will not significantly affect your hosting costs, provided we use the right caching strategy (ISR + On-Demand Revalidation).**

Here is exactly how we will architect this so your Next.js hosting costs stay near **zero**, even if gold prices fluctuate wildly and trigger updates every 5 minutes.

### The Trap to Avoid: Server-Side Rendering (SSR)
If you tell Next.js to fetch the gold rate from Django *every single time a user visits a product page* (using `export const dynamic = 'force-dynamic'`), you are paying for server compute on every page view. If 10,000 people visit your site, you pay for 10,000 database queries and compute cycles. **This is how Vercel bills get out of hand.**

### The Solution: ISR + On-Demand Revalidation
Instead, we will use **Incremental Static Regeneration (ISR)**. Next.js will pre-build the product pages into static HTML (which costs $0 to serve) and cache them on the global Edge Network. 

Here is the exact flow we will build for your Gold Rate feature:

1. **The Background Job (Django):** A cron job (via GitHub Actions or Celery) runs every 30 minutes. It checks the live gold API. If the rate hasn't changed, it does nothing (Cost: $0).
2. **The Update (Django):** If the rate *has* changed, Django updates the `RateCard` in your database.
3. **The Trigger (Django ➔ Next.js):** Django sends a tiny, secure webhook to your Next.js app: `POST /api/revalidate?secret=xyz`.
4. **The Cache Purge (Next.js):** Next.js receives the webhook and instantly purges the cache for your product pages using `revalidateTag('products')`. 
5. **The Rebuild (Next.js):** The *next* person who visits a product page triggers a background rebuild. Next.js fetches the new gold rate from Django, calculates the new prices, and caches the new HTML. 

### Why this is incredibly cheap:
* **Serving cached HTML:** Free.
* **The Revalidation Compute:** Rebuilding a cached page takes Next.js about **50 to 100 milliseconds** of serverless compute time. Vercel charges by the second (rounded up). Revalidating 1,000 product pages after a gold rate change will cost you a fraction of a single cent.
* **The User Experience:** The user never waits. The first user sees the slightly stale price (while the background rebuild happens), and every user after that sees the lightning-fast, newly cached price.

Since we are migrating to Next.js, we will structure the new frontend specifically to handle this. 

**What stays the same:**
* Your Django backend remains the single source of truth for Inventory (`ProductInstance`), Users, Orders, and the `RateCard`.
* Your Postgres database remains untouched.

**What changes:**
